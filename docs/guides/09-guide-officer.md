## The job

**Command is a job, not a prize.**

The rank means you're responsible for people — their training, their progression, their experience of this community, and whether they log in next week. It does not mean you've earned deference, better gear, or the right to win arguments.

> **The measure of a good officer here is not how well you perform in an operation. It is whether your people are still here in six months, and whether one of them is ready to take your job.**

---

## The two rules you cannot break

**1. The firewall.** Command rank grants **zero** staff powers. You cannot ban, kick, mute, or warn anyone. Rule violations get reported to staff — you never handle them in character.

Where they overlap: a trooper who repeatedly RDMs is both a rule violation *and* a performance issue. Report it to staff **and** counsel them. Two tracks, both run.

**2. Management doesn't command.** No member of Management plays above Warrant Officer. If you're in Management, you're a Sergeant. This is structural and not waivable — the moment the owner sits at the top of the IC chain, every decision becomes unappealable and honest feedback stops.

## Running promotion boards

Required for every promotion to **Sergeant and above.**

| Promoting to | Board |
|---|---|
| SGT / SSGT | 3 members: 1 officer (chair), 1 SSGT+, 1 NCO from another battalion |
| WO / 2LT / 1LT | 3 members: 1 field grade (chair), 2 officers, 1+ external |
| CPT and above | 5 members: 1 High Command (chair), 3 officers, 1 external |

**At least one external member on every board.** This is what stops battalions becoming closed loops that promote their own regardless of quality. Don't waive it.

**Recuse yourself** if you're close to the candidate. Same standard as staff.

### What you're scoring

| Area | Weight |
|---|---|
| Knowledge — SOP, comms, ROE, chain of command | 25% |
| **Leadership judgment** — situational scenarios | **35%** |
| Record — hours, quals, ops, counseling history | 20% |
| **Peer and subordinate input** | **20%** |

**Ask the people they lead.** An officer beloved by the board and dreaded by their troopers is exactly the officer we must not promote. Solicit that input privately, and weight it.

### Deferral is the default for "close but not ready"

A deferral must come with **specific written development items, a named mentor, and a re-board date** — usually 30 days. Say out loud in the room that a deferral is not a failure. It changes how they hear it.

**Written feedback within 48 hours, always — including on a pass.**

## Discipline

**Praise publicly. Correct privately. Always.**

| Step | Action |
|---|---|
| 1 | Verbal counseling |
| 2 | Written counseling, with a specific improvement plan |
| 3 | Extra duty (max 3 sessions) |
| 4 | Qualification suspension |
| 5 | Reduction in rank — **requires a board, appealable, trooper may bring an advocate** |
| 6 | Removal from unit — requires High Command |

### Hard rules

- **No mass punishment.** Banned outright.
- **No punishment while angry.** Wait a session.
- **Every counseling names a behavior and a fix.** "Do better" isn't counseling.
- **All command discipline is appealable** — up the chain, or to the Ombudsman if the chain is the problem.
- **Records expire** after 90 days clean.

### Insubordination — what it isn't

Refusing an unlawful order, asking a clarifying question, and raising a concern before executing are **never** insubordination.

**Train your people to speak up.** An officer who punishes dissent gets surrounded by silence, and silence is how operations fail.

## Running your unit

**Weekly minimums:** one training session (hosted or delegated) · roster maintenance · one promotion candidate in development · 8 hours presence. **Monthly:** one operation written or led.

### Roster

| Status | Threshold | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Active | 3+ hrs / 14 days | — |
| Low activity | Under 3 hrs / 14 days | Check-in message |
| Inactive | Under 1 hr / 21 days, no LOA | Removal notice, 7 days to respond |
| LOA | Declared | Held up to 60 days |

**LOA is free, unlimited, and needs no reason.** People have jobs, school, and lives. A unit that punishes absence loses the people worth keeping.

## Succession — the thing you'll skip

**Every command position must have a designated *and trained* second.**

If you vanished tomorrow, would your unit function? If not, that's your failure to fix, this week.

- Designate a 2IC when you take command
- Your 2IC runs **at least one training and one operation per month** — designated-but-untrained is worthless
- Handover on departure is written: roster state, ongoing issues, promotion pipeline, unit traditions

**Term limits:** battalion command is 4 months, renewable once. Stepping down at term end carries no stigma — most former commanders move into instructor or advisory roles, which are genuinely valuable and far less exhausting.

## Briefing an operation

The five-paragraph order. Five minutes. It's the difference between a coordinated op and forty people wandering.

1. **Situation** — enemy, friendlies, what's happening
2. **Mission** — one sentence: who, what, when, where, why
3. **Execution** — your intent, scheme of maneuver, tasks by element
4. **Sustainment** — ammo, medical, extraction
5. **Command & Signal** — chain of command, nets, callsigns

**Then run the AAR.** Ten minutes, four questions: What was supposed to happen? What happened? What went well? What changes?

**No rank in the AAR.** A CT may tell you your plan was bad. An AAR where nobody criticizes anything is a wasted AAR — and if that's happening every week, it's a sign your people don't feel safe telling you things.

## Recognition

The most underused retention tool you have, and it's free.

Present awards **in formation, out loud, with a specific reason.** *"For pulling two casualties out under fire at Sullust"* beats a Discord ping every time.

**Recognize the unglamorous.** The trooper who ran the armory post reliably for three months has done more for this server than the one who got a lucky kill in an op. Build your award practice around that and watch what it does to your retention.

## Weekly checklist

1. Did I correct anyone publicly? *(Don't.)*
2. Did anyone in my unit go a week without me speaking to them by name?
3. **Is my 2IC ready to run this without me?**
4. Who's closest to promotion, and what's specifically holding them back?
5. Did I ask a subordinate for honest feedback and actually listen?
6. Did anyone leave this month, and do I know why?
7. **Am I still having fun?** If no, take an LOA before you burn out and take three people with you.
