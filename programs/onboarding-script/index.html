    ONBOARDING SCRIPT¶

    [SERVER NAME] — Boot camp, run cold off the page¶

    Instructor-facing · 15 minutes · Delivers the Basic Rifleman qualification

    Before you start¶

    This is the fifteen minutes that decides whether this person comes back. Not the Saturday op, not the campaign — this. Treat it accordingly.
    Duration 15 minutes. Hard stop.
    Class size 1–6. Above 6, run two sessions.
    Location Simulation deck, range configuration
    You need Range targets loaded, a spare rifle, a sponsor standing by
    Outcome Basic Rifleman qualification + assigned sponsor + assigned fire team

    Nobody fails this course. Two attempts, then you work with them one-on-one until they pass. This is the one gate that never gates.

    Say things in your own words. The scripted lines below are floor, not ceiling — they exist so you never have to think about what comes next.

    MINUTE 0–2 — Meet them¶

    "Welcome aboard. I'm Sergeant [name], I'll be running you through basics. Fifteen minutes, then you're with your unit. What do I call you?"

    They don't have a name yet — they're a CT with a number. Use the number. It's not an insult, it's the setup for the payoff later.

    Do: get their name/number, ask if they've played GMod RP before, ask if they're on mic.

    Don't: open with rules. Don't open with lore. Don't say "before we begin, read the Charter."

    "You've got a number right now, not a name. You earn one. Usually your squad gives it to you and usually it's about something embarrassing. That's tradition, don't fight it."

    If they're not on mic: that's fine, say so explicitly. "No mic is fine, plenty of people run text. You'll want one eventually for ops but nobody's going to hassle you."

    MINUTE 2–5 — Courtesies and chain of command¶

    Keep this fast. It's the least fun part and they'll absorb it by doing, not by hearing.

    Demonstrate, then have them do it:

    1. The salute. "Officers only — anyone Second Lieutenant and up. First time you see them each session."
    2. Reporting. "Sir, CT-2247 reporting as ordered." Have them say it out loud once.
    3. Acknowledging. "'Yes sir,' 'copy,' or 'wilco.' Not 'ok,' not 'yeah.'"

    Then the one that actually matters:

    "Bearing is situational. In formation, on an op, at your post, on the bridge — discipline. In the barracks, in the mess, off duty — be as loud as you want. Knowing which room you're in is the whole skill. Nobody here wants you standing at attention in the mess hall."

    And this, explicitly:

    "If you think an order is wrong, say so before you do it, then do it. 'Sir, recommend we go east instead.' That's not insubordination and nobody will punish you for it. If somebody does, report it — that's on them, not you."

    Say that out loud even though it's in the guide. It's the single line that most changes how someone behaves in their first month.

    MINUTE 5–8 — Weapons handling¶

    Hands on the rifle. Talking stops.

    Demo Then they do it
    Draw and holster Both, twice
    Reload Once
    Weapons Cold — holstered Call it, they comply
    Weapons Tight — drawn, fire on positive ID Call it, they comply
    Weapons Free — fire at discretion Call it, they comply

    "Cold is the base default on this ship. If you're walking around with a rifle out and nobody's called Amber, someone's going to say something."

    Call the three states in random order and watch them respond. That's the assessment — takes thirty seconds and it sticks better than an explanation.

    MINUTE 8–11 — Comms¶

    This is the highest-value block in the course. If you're running short on time, cut somewhere else.

    Write it out or say it slowly, twice:

    "[Them], this is [me], [message], over."

    Have each of them transmit one call to you. Any content. Correct the format once, kindly, and move on.

    Then the five words:
    Over Done, respond
    Out Done, no response
    Copy / Roger Got it
    Wilco Got it, doing it
    Say again Repeat — never say "repeat"

    Then the three that interrupt everything:

    "Check fire. Man down. Contact. These three stop whatever else is happening on the net. If you hear 'check fire,' you stop shooting immediately — not after one more shot."

    Have them say each of the three out loud once. Muscle memory for the mouth.

    MINUTE 11–14 — The range¶

    Ten targets, close and medium. Six hits to pass.

    Before they fire:

    "Positive ID before you shoot. Check what's behind your target. If I call check fire, you stop instantly."

    Call "check fire" once during the run, unannounced, mid-string. That's the real test. Someone who keeps shooting gets one correction and one retry — and now they'll never forget it.

    Score it out loud. "Eight of ten, and you stopped on check fire. That's a pass."

    If they miss the standard: run it again immediately. Don't make it a thing. "Range nerves, everyone gets them. Go again."

    MINUTE 14–15 — Hand off¶

    This is the part everyone skips and it's the part that determines retention.

    1. Award the qualification out loud. "CT-2247, you're Basic Rifleman qualified. That's logged. Congratulations."
    2. Introduce the sponsor by name, in person. Not a Discord link. Not "someone will find you." A person, standing there.

      "This is Corporal [name]. He's your sponsor. Anything you're confused about for the next two weeks, ask him — that's literally his job and he gets credit for it."

    3. Sponsor walks them to their battalion physically.
    4. Name their fire team members. Three or four people, by name.
    5. Give them one concrete next goal:

      "Get ten hours of duty time and a second qualification and you'll make Specialist. Medic and Comms are the easiest to pick up and the most useful to your squad. Marksman's the most fun."

    6. Point at the card. "Everything from today is on the Trooper Card. Keep it open."

    Then stop. Don't add more. They're full.

    Failure modes — the things that lose people¶

    Don't Why
    Run over 15 minutes The single most common killer. They came to play.
    Lecture on lore Nobody has ever stayed for a lore lecture
    Say "go read the rules in Discord" They won't, and now they think we're bureaucratic
    Leave them without a sponsor The highest-correlation predictor of them not returning
    Haze, test patience, or make them stand at attention as a filter Immediate command discipline matter. Also just wrong.
    Spawn them into an empty map with no marker Fix the spawn, not the trooper
    Be visibly bored They can tell. It tells them what this place is like.

    Instructor self-check¶

    After each session:

    1. Did I finish inside 15 minutes?
    2. Did they physically meet their sponsor and fire team?
    3. Did they transmit at least one radio call themselves?
    4. Did they leave with one specific next goal?
    5. Did they talk at all? If they said nothing for fifteen minutes, I ran a lecture, not a course.
