# EVENT PLANNER PLAYBOOK
### [SERVER NAME] — Build a decent operation in 45 minutes
*For the Event Planner corps · Templates, structure, and the rules that stop burnout*

---

## Why this document exists

**One person writing every operation is the single most common way a server dies.** They produce brilliant work for three months, burn out, quit, and the server has no content pipeline. It's happened to hundreds of communities.

This playbook exists so an operation takes **45 minutes to build, not six hours**, which is what makes a six-person planner corps possible instead of a one-person heroic effort.

---

## Corps rules

| Rule | Standard |
|---|---|
| **Minimum planners** | 6. Below 4, recruit before you schedule. |
| **Saturday main op lead** | **Rotates weekly.** Nobody runs two in a row. |
| **Ops per planner** | Maximum 2 per month |
| **Lead time** | Template picked 5 days out, brief written 2 days out |
| **Approval** | Thursday minor ops: none. Saturday main ops: one other planner reads it. |
| **LOA** | Free, unlimited, no reason required |

**If you're dreading writing it, hand it off.** A traded op is normal. A resented op is visible to everyone playing it.

## The 45-minute build

| Minutes | Do |
|---|---|
| **0–5** | Pick a template. Pick a campaign system it's fought over. |
| **5–15** | Fill the five-paragraph order. |
| **15–25** | Place the map — spawns, objectives, enemy positions. |
| **25–35** | Set enemy composition and waves from the table below. |
| **35–40** | Write the two branch outcomes: what changes on a win, what changes on a loss. |
| **40–45** | Post the brief. Assign element leads. |

**Don't write a story.** Write a situation and let the players make the story. Every hour you spend on narrative is an hour that produces less fun than an hour spent on the map.

## The five templates

Every operation on this server is one of these five, reskinned. That is a feature.

### T1 — DEFENSE
*Hold a position against escalating waves.*

**Best for:** large turnouts, mixed skill levels, new players. Hardest to run badly.
**Structure:** friendlies fortify → 3–5 waves of increasing pressure → final push → hold or fall back.
**Win:** position held at timer.
**Watch for:** waves that arrive from the same direction every time. Rotate the axis.

### T2 — ASSAULT
*Take a defended objective.*

**Best for:** coordinated groups, 20+ players, showcasing combined arms.
**Structure:** insertion → advance under contact → breach → clear → hold against counterattack.
**Win:** objective held for 5 minutes after clearing.
**Watch for:** a single chokepoint turning it into a meat grinder. Always build two approaches.

### T3 — BOARDING
*Fight through a hostile vessel, or repel boarders on our own.*

**Best for:** medium turnouts, CQB, 104th and Coruscant Guard showcases.
**Structure:** breach the airlock → clear compartments → reach the objective (bridge, reactor, prisoner) → extract.
**Win:** objective secured, extraction with acceptable casualties.
**Watch for:** corridors so tight that only the front two players get to shoot. Use parallel routes.

### T4 — ESCORT
*Move something fragile from A to B.*

**Best for:** armor and transport showcases, teaching coordination, medium turnouts.
**Structure:** link up → move along a route → 2–3 ambushes → arrival.
**Win:** the package survives.
**Watch for:** the convoy outrunning its infantry. Build in forced halts.

### T5 — RECON
*Find something without being found.*

**Best for:** small turnouts (8–15), 91st showcases, weeknights, when you want something different.
**Structure:** insert quietly → locate the objective → observe and report → exfiltrate, contact optional.
**Win:** intelligence gathered and reported. **Contact is a partial failure, not a fail.**
**Watch for:** players who want to shoot things. Brief the intent clearly or they'll turn it into T2.

## Enemy composition

Scale by turnout. These are starting points — adjust after your first run.

| Turnout | B1 | B2 | Droideka | Commando | Vehicle |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8–15 | 25–40 | 4–6 | 1 | 2 | 0–1 |
| 15–25 | 40–70 | 8–12 | 2 | 3–4 | 1 |
| 25–40 | 70–120 | 15–20 | 3–4 | 5–6 | 2 |

**Composition rules:**
- **B1s are the volume.** They should die easily and constantly. That's the fun.
- **B2s are pressure.** They make players use cover.
- **Droidekas are events.** One droideka should change the shape of a fight. Never spawn four at once.
- **Commando droids are threats.** Use them to make players nervous, not to kill people.
- **Tactical droids are objectives.** A named enemy commander gives the op a face.

**Escalate in waves, not in one lump.** Three waves of 20 beats one wave of 60 in every measurable way.

## The brief

Five-paragraph order, five minutes at muster. **Never longer.**

1. **Situation** — enemy, friendlies, what's going on
2. **Mission** — one sentence: who, what, when, where, why
3. **Execution** — commander's intent, scheme of maneuver, tasks by element
4. **Sustainment** — ammo, medical, extraction
5. **Command & Signal** — chain of command, nets, callsigns

**Give the ground commander the mission, then get out of the way.** You built the situation. They fight it. Resist the urge to correct their plan mid-op because it isn't the one you imagined.

## Campaign integration

Every operation is fought over a named system on the campaign map, and **you write both outcomes before you run it.**

| | Example |
|---|---|
| **On a win** | Control of [system] +2. Supply line to [adjacent] opens. Armory restocks heavy weapons. |
| **On a loss** | Control −1. Enemy pressure on [adjacent] increases. Two fighters unavailable next week. |

**Write the loss branch honestly.** An operation the players cannot lose has no stakes, and they can tell. Losing a system and having to take it back three weeks later produces better stories than winning every Saturday.

## Running it

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Set up the map before muster | Build while players wait |
| Announce the timer at the start | Let it run 90 minutes because it's going well |
| Let the ground commander command | Rescript mid-op because they surprised you |
| Adjust enemy spawns live if it's lopsided | Add enemies because players are doing well |
| Call ENDEX cleanly | Let it fizzle out |
| Run the AAR immediately | Skip it because everyone's tired |

**Target duration: 60 minutes.** 90 is the ceiling. Ending while people still want more is the correct feeling to leave them with.

---

## The AAR

Ten minutes. Four questions. **No rank in the AAR** — a CT may tell a Colonel the plan was bad, and may tell you the op was bad.

1. What was supposed to happen?
2. What actually happened?
3. What went well?
4. What do we change?

**Log the answers.** They feed the campaign map and the next planner's build.

---

## After the op — five minutes

Post to the planner channel:

> Template used · System fought over · Turnout · Duration · Outcome and campaign effect · **One thing that worked · One thing that didn't**

**That last pair is the whole library.** Six planners each contributing two lines a month builds an institutional memory that no single person could hold, and it's what makes the seventh planner good on their first attempt.

---

## Burnout check

1. Am I building more than 2 ops a month?
2. Have I led two Saturdays in a row?
3. Am I dreading this one?
4. Did I take a month off in the last six?

**Any yes: hand it off or take the LOA.** The corps exists so that's a normal move, not a failure.
