    NCO & INSTRUCTOR GUIDE¶

    [SERVER NAME] — Corporal through Warrant Officer¶

    Role guide 2 of 4 · Drawn from the Command Handbook and Qualification Syllabi

    The job¶

    You are the layer where this server actually works.

    Officers set direction. Staff handle rules. NCOs handle people — training them, sponsoring them, correcting them, and noticing when someone's about to quit. Almost every member who stays does so because an NCO made them feel like they belonged in week one.

    Your measure isn't your own performance in an op. It's whether your people are still here in three months.

    Leading a fire team¶

    Three to five troopers. You own their training, their comms discipline, and their morale.

    Weekly Do this
    Talk to each of them by name If someone goes a full week without being addressed directly, that's a miss
    Run one short drill 15 minutes of CQB or comms practice beats an hour-long lecture
    Know who's closest to a promotion And know the specific thing holding them back
    Notice absences Someone who quietly stops logging in is someone you could have kept

    In an op: you are the voice your team hears. Keep your net clean, call your movements, and get your casualties to the CCP. If your medic goes down because you didn't cover them, that's on you.

    Sponsoring a new trooper¶

    You'll be assigned recruits. This is the highest-leverage thing you do.

    1. Meet them in their first session. Not later. Now.
    2. Walk them to their battalion physically. Don't link them a channel.
    3. Introduce them to their fire team by name.
    4. Check in after their second session. This is the one everyone skips and it's the one that matters.
    5. Push them toward their second qualification within two weeks.

    Sponsorship counts toward your own promotion, and five sponsored recruits reaching Specialist earns the Sponsor Ribbon. That's deliberate — we pay for this work because it's the work that keeps the server alive.

    Running a tryout¶

    Rule Standard
    Duration 30 minutes maximum
    Notice 24 hours minimum, announced publicly
    Minimum attendance One. Never cancel on a single candidate — they made time for you
    Rubric Published before the tryout. They should know exactly what's measured
    Result Same session, in writing
    Retry 7 days. No lifetime bars, ever

    Standard structure: bearing (5) → knowledge check (5) → marksmanship (5) → practical scenario (10) → interview (5).

    Absolutely prohibited¶

    • Hazing. Any form. Not tradition, not character-building, not "how it was done for me." This is a command discipline matter and probably a Charter violation.
    • Endurance filtering. Standing people at attention for 40 minutes measures patience, not aptitude, and selects for exactly the wrong people.
    • Vague failure. "Not ready" is not feedback. Tell them the specific thing and the date they can retry.

    Certifying (Instructor qualification)¶

    You can only certify in qualifications you personally hold.

    The method — all four phases, every time:

    Explain → Demonstrate → Practice → Assess

    Lecture alone doesn't work. If you're talking for more than a third of the course, restructure it.

    The instructor code¶

    1. You are not the point. The student is.
    2. Score the published standard, not your personal preference.
    3. Never fail someone without telling them exactly how to pass.
    4. No hazing. Ever. Any form. Any reason.
    5. Stay under the time limit — every time you run long, your next class is smaller.
    6. If everyone fails, the course is broken, not the students.
    7. Recognize the biggest improvement, not just the highest score.

    Log every certification: candidate, qual, date, instructor, score by block, pass/defer/fail, written feedback.

    Stay current: one course per month minimum. Student feedback is collected anonymously and reviewed.

    Correcting people¶

    Praise publicly. Correct privately. Always. No exceptions.

    Step When
    Verbal counseling First time. Private conversation, noted in the file.
    Written counseling Pattern. Documented, signed, includes a specific improvement plan and a date.
    Escalate to your officer Beyond that — extra duty, qual suspension, and rank reduction aren't yours to issue

    Hard rules¶

    • No mass punishment. Punishing a squad for one person's mistake is banned. It builds resentment and drives people off the server.
    • Never correct while angry. Wait a session. Nothing is that urgent.
    • "Do better" is not counseling. Name the behavior, name the fix.
    • Rule violations go to staff, not you. Cheating, harassment, RDM — report it. You handle performance; staff handle rules. Don't blur that line.

    The firewall¶

    If you also hold a staff position: your staff rank gives you zero additional in-character authority, and your NCO rank gives you zero moderation authority.

    When it matters, say which hat you're wearing out loud. And never use staff tools in a dispute you're personally part of — call another staff member. Every time.

    Your weekly checklist¶

    1. Did I correct anyone publicly this week? (Don't.)
    2. Did anyone on my team go a week without me speaking to them by name?
    3. Who's closest to a promotion, and what specifically is in the way?
    4. Did I run at least one drill?
    5. Have I checked on my sponsored recruits?
    6. Did anyone leave, and do I know why?
    7. Am I still enjoying this? If no, take an LOA before you burn out and take three people with you.
