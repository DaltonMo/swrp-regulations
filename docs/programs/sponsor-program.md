# SPONSOR PROGRAM
### [SERVER NAME] — Pairing every new trooper with a person
*One page · The highest-leverage retention mechanic we have, and it costs nothing but policy*

---

## Why

**People stay for people.**

A new member who has one named person to ask questions of returns at a dramatically higher rate than one who has a Discord channel and a rulebook. That's the entire theory, and it's the reason this program is written down and rewarded rather than left to good intentions.

**Every recruit gets a sponsor. Within their first session. Every time.**

---

## Who can sponsor

| Requirement | Standard |
|---|---|
| **Rank** | Corporal or above |
| **Tenure** | 30 days on the server |
| **Record** | No active counseling |
| **Load** | Maximum 2 active sponsees at a time |
| **Availability** | Reasonably active during the recruit's play hours |

**Match on time zone and play hours before anything else.** A great sponsor who is never online when the recruit is is worse than an average one who is.

---

## What a sponsor does

| When | Action |
|---|---|
| **First session** | Meet them at the end of boot camp, **in person**. Walk them to their battalion. Introduce their fire team by name. |
| **First session, before they log** | Get one contact point — Discord handle, so they exist to you outside the server. |
| **Second session** | **Check in.** This is the one everyone skips and it's the one that matters most. |
| **Week 1** | Take them into one activity — a sim run, a patrol, a drill. Anything with other people in it. |
| **Week 2** | Push them toward their second qualification. Sit their range run if you can. |
| **Week 3–4** | Introduce them to someone outside their fire team. Widening the circle is what makes them stay past month one. |
| **End of week 4** | Sponsorship formally ends. Say so, and say they can still ask you anything. |

**Total time cost: roughly 30 minutes a week for four weeks.**

---

## What a sponsor is not

- **Not their commander.** You may not outrank them for long. Advice, not orders.
- **Not staff.** Rule violations go to staff. If your sponsee is cheating, report it — sponsorship is not cover.
- **Not a trainer.** Instructors certify. You point them at the course and make sure they show up.
- **Not permanent.** Four weeks. Then they're just a friend, which is the point.

---

## Rewards

| Reward | Earned by |
|---|---|
| **Credits** | Per sponsee reaching Specialist |
| **Sponsor Ribbon** | 5 sponsees reaching Specialist |
| **Promotion credit** | Sponsorship counts toward NCO and officer promotion criteria |

**We pay for this because it's the work that keeps the server alive**, and unpaid invisible work reliably stops happening. A promotion board should ask an NCO candidate how many people they've sponsored and how those people are doing.

---

## Tracking

One row per pairing. A spreadsheet is fine at launch; it moves into the personnel file system later.

> Recruit · Sponsor · Start date · Second-session check-in (Y/N) · Second qual date · Still active at 30 days (Y/N)

**The only metric that matters: 30-day retention rate for sponsored vs. unsponsored recruits.** If a recruit ever goes unsponsored, that's an accidental control group — note it. Within two months you'll know exactly what this program is worth, in numbers.

---

## When it fails

**Sponsor goes inactive:** the battalion NCO reassigns within 48 hours. Don't wait for the recruit to notice they've been abandoned.

**Bad match:** either party can request a swap, no reason required, no hard feelings. Say this out loud when you assign, so neither of them feels stuck.

**Recruit goes quiet:** one message. *"Hey, haven't seen you on — everything alright?"* No pressure, no guilt, no second message. Some people just move on, and chasing them makes the server feel needy.

**No sponsor available:** an officer takes it personally until one frees up. A recruit without a sponsor is a hole in the process, not an acceptable outcome.
