## 0. What Command Is

Command is a **job**, not a prize.

The rank on your shoulder means you are responsible for people. Not that you have earned deference, not that you get better gear, not that you win arguments. Responsible for people — their training, their progression, their experience of this community, and whether they log in next week.

**The measure of a good officer on this server is not how well they perform in an operation. It is whether their people are still here in six months and whether one of them is ready to take their job.**

---

## 1. The Firewall (Restated)

> **Command rank grants zero staff powers. Staff rank grants zero command authority.**

- A Marshal Commander cannot ban, kick, mute, or warn anyone. Ever.
- Rule violations get reported to staff. **You do not handle them in character.**
- If a trooper cheats, you don't demote them — you report it. If a trooper is a poor squad leader, you don't ban them — you counsel them.
- **Command discipline is for in-character performance. Staff discipline is for rule violations.** Do not blur these.

**Where they overlap:** a trooper who repeatedly RDMs during operations is both a rule violation (staff) and a performance issue (command). Report it to staff *and* counsel them. Two tracks, both run.

---

## 2. The Founder Rule

**No member of Management plays a character above the rank of Warrant Officer.**

This is a structural rule and it is not waivable.

The moment the owner also sits at the top of the in-character chain, three things happen immediately and irreversibly: every IC decision becomes unappealable, subordinates stop giving honest feedback, and the server becomes one person's story rather than a shared one.

Management plays sergeants, medics, and pilots. That is not a sacrifice — it is where the good roleplay actually is.

## 3. Rank Structure

### 3.1 Enlisted

| Rank | Abbrev | Role | Time in Grade | Requirements |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Clone Trooper | CT | Recruit | — | Complete boot camp |
| Trooper Specialist | CT-S | Qualified trooper | 10 hrs | Basic Rifleman + 1 additional qual |
| Corporal | CPL | Fire team 2IC | 20 hrs | 3 quals, sponsor recommendation |
| Sergeant | SGT | Fire team leader | 40 hrs | 4 quals, board, leads a fire team |
| Staff Sergeant | SSGT | Squad leader | 70 hrs | 5 quals incl. Instructor, board |
| Warrant Officer | WO | Technical specialist | 100 hrs | Deep specialization, board |

### 3.2 Commissioned

| Rank | Abbrev | Role | Time in Grade | Requirements |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Second Lieutenant | 2LT | Platoon leader | 100 hrs | Board + Officer Course |
| First Lieutenant | 1LT | Senior platoon leader | 140 hrs | Board, demonstrated leadership |
| Captain | CPT | Company commander | 200 hrs | Board, has trained a subordinate to SGT |
| Major | MAJ | Battalion executive officer | 280 hrs | Board, High Command endorsement |
| Lieutenant Colonel | LTC | Battalion 2IC | 360 hrs | Board, High Command endorsement |
| Colonel | COL | Battalion commander | 450 hrs | Appointment by High Command |
| Commander | CMDR | Multi-battalion command | — | Appointment, term-limited |
| Marshal Commander | MCMDR | GAR ground forces | — | Appointment, term-limited |

**Time in Grade is a floor, not an entitlement.** Meeting the hours does not create a right to promotion. Nobody is owed a rank for showing up.

### 3.3 Rank Caps

Ranks are **capped by roster size** to prevent rank inflation — the disease that turns a server into forty officers commanding six privates.

| Roster size | Max COL | Max MAJ/LTC | Max CPT | Max officers total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Under 30 | 1 per battalion | 1 | 2 | 15% of roster |
| 30–60 | 1 per battalion | 2 | 4 | 15% of roster |
| 60+ | 1 per battalion | 3 | 6 | 15% of roster |

**When a rank is capped, promotion requires a vacancy.** This is a feature. Scarcity is what makes rank mean something.

## 4. Promotion Boards

### 4.1 When a Board Is Required

**All promotions to Sergeant and above.** Below Sergeant, a battalion commander may promote directly on meeting the criteria.

### 4.2 Composition

| Promoting to | Board composition |
|---|---|
| SGT / SSGT | 3 members: 1 officer (chair), 1 SSGT+, 1 NCO from another battalion |
| WO / 2LT / 1LT | 3 members: 1 field grade officer (chair), 2 officers, at least 1 external |
| CPT and above | 5 members: 1 High Command (chair), 3 officers, 1 external |

**"External" means from a different battalion than the candidate.** At least one external member on every board. This is what prevents battalions becoming closed loops that promote their own regardless of quality.

**Recusal:** any board member with a close personal relationship to the candidate recuses. Same standard as staff.

### 4.3 Process

1. **Nomination** — by the candidate's direct superior, or self-nomination with a sponsoring NCO/officer
2. **Packet submission** — personnel file: hours, quals, sponsor statement, any counseling records
3. **Board convenes** — scheduled, candidate present, 20–30 minutes
4. **Examination** — see §4.4
5. **Deliberation** — candidate steps out
6. **Ruling** — Promote / Defer with development plan / Deny with reasons
7. **Written feedback to the candidate within 48 hours, always**, including on a pass

### 4.4 Board Content

| Area | Weight | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Knowledge** | 25% | SOP, comms procedure, ROE, chain of command, unit lore |
| **Leadership** | 35% | Situational judgment: "Your squad lead goes down mid-objective. Walk me through the next 60 seconds." |
| **Record** | 20% | Duty hours, op participation, quals, counseling history |
| **Peer standing** | 20% | Input from peers and subordinates, solicited privately |

**Peer and subordinate input is mandatory and weighted.** An officer who is beloved by the board and dreaded by their troopers is exactly the officer we must not promote. Ask the people they lead.

### 4.5 Deferral

Deferral is the default for a candidate who is close but not ready. A deferral must come with:
- Specific, written development items
- A named mentor
- A re-board date (typically 30 days)

**Deferral is not a failure.** Frame it that way explicitly, out loud, in the room.

## 5. Tryouts

### 5.1 Standards

| Requirement | Standard |
|---|---|
| **Duration** | 30 minutes maximum |
| **Notice** | Announced at least 24 hours in advance |
| **Minimum attendees** | 1 (never cancel on a single candidate — that person made time) |
| **Host** | SGT or above with Instructor qualification |
| **Grading** | Published rubric, scored, shown to the candidate |
| **Result** | Delivered same session, in writing |

### 5.2 Rules

1. **Publish the rubric before the tryout.** Candidates should know exactly what's being measured.
2. **No hazing.** None. Not "tradition," not "building character," not "how it was done for me." Any hazing is an immediate command discipline matter and likely a Charter violation.
3. **No endurance filtering.** Making people stand at attention for 40 minutes measures patience, not aptitude, and it selects for the wrong people.
4. **Failure gets specific feedback and a retry date**, never a vague "not ready."
5. **Retry cooldown: 7 days.** No lifetime bars from a unit.
6. **Cancelled tryouts must be re-announced within 7 days.** Repeated cancellation is a leadership failure.

### 5.3 Standard Tryout Structure

| Phase | Time | Measures |
|---|---|---|
| Formation & bearing | 5 min | Discipline, presence |
| Knowledge check | 5 min | SOP, comms, ROE |
| Marksmanship | 5 min | Range score |
| Practical scenario | 10 min | Judgment, comms, teamwork |
| Interview | 5 min | Motivation, fit, availability |

## 6. In-Character Discipline

### 6.1 Scope

Command discipline addresses **performance and conduct in character**: dereliction of a post, insubordination, negligence with a qualified capability, repeated tardiness, failure of leadership responsibility.

**It does not address rule violations.** Those go to staff. Always.

### 6.2 Progressive Ladder

| Step | Action | Record |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Verbal counseling** — private, corrective conversation | Noted in file |
| 2 | **Written counseling** — documented, signed by both, includes a specific improvement plan | In file, 90 days |
| 3 | **Extra duty** — additional watch rotations, max 3 sessions | In file, 90 days |
| 4 | **Qualification suspension** — pull a qual after negligence; requires recertification | In file, until recert |
| 5 | **Reduction in rank** — one grade, requires a board, appealable | Permanent record |
| 6 | **Removal from unit** — transfer to another battalion or to unassigned; requires High Command | Permanent record |

### 6.3 Rules of Discipline

1. **Praise publicly, correct privately.** Always. No exceptions.
2. **No mass punishment.** Punishing a squad for one person's mistake builds resentment and drives people off the server. It is banned.
3. **No punishment while angry.** Wait a session.
4. **Counseling requires a fix.** Every counseling names a specific behavior and a specific improvement. "Do better" is not counseling.
5. **Reduction in rank requires a board**, and the trooper may bring an advocate.
6. **All command discipline is appealable** to the next level of command, and to the Ombudsman if the chain itself is the problem.
7. **Records expire.** Verbal and written counseling drop off after 90 days of clean performance.

### 6.4 Insubordination

Refusing a lawful order in character is a disciplinary matter. **But three things are never insubordination:**

- Refusing an order that violates the Charter
- Asking a clarifying question
- Raising a concern before executing ("Sir, recommend we approach from the east instead — request permission to advise")

**Train your people to speak up.** An officer who punishes dissent will be surrounded by silence, and silence is how operations fail.

## 7. Battalion Command Duties

### 7.1 Weekly Minimums

Every battalion commander is responsible for:

- **One training session per week**, hosted or delegated
- **Roster maintenance** — activity check, LOA processing, removals
- **Promotion pipeline** — at least one candidate developed toward a board
- **One operation contribution per month** — write or lead
- **Presence** — minimum 8 hours/week on the server

### 7.2 Roster Management

| Status | Definition | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Active** | 3+ hours in the last 14 days | — |
| **Low activity** | Under 3 hours in 14 days | Check-in message |
| **Inactive** | Under 1 hour in 21 days, no LOA | Removal notice, 7 days to respond |
| **LOA** | Declared absence | Held indefinitely, up to 60 days |
| **Retired** | Voluntary departure | Rank held for 30 days on return |

**LOA is free, unlimited, and requires no reason.** Post it and go. People have jobs, school, deployments, and lives. A community that punishes absence loses the people worth keeping.

### 7.3 Succession

**Every command position must have a designated and *trained* second.**

If you are a battalion commander and you disappeared tomorrow, your unit should function. If it wouldn't, that is your failure to fix, this week.

- Designate a 2IC on assuming command
- 2IC runs at least one training session and one operation per month
- Handover on departure includes a written brief: roster state, ongoing issues, promotion pipeline, unit traditions

## 8. Command Term Limits

| Position | Term | Renewal |
|---|---|---|
| Battalion Commander (COL) | 4 months | Renewable once, then 2-month step-down |
| Commander (CMDR) | 4 months | Renewable once |
| Marshal Commander | 4 months | Renewable once |
| Jedi Council | 6 months | Renewable once |

**Why:** term limits give leaders a graceful exit that isn't quitting in frustration, they force succession planning to be real, and they prevent the ossification that turns leadership into a permanent clique.

**Stepping down at term end carries no stigma.** Former commanders retain their rank and typically move into instructor or advisory roles, which are genuinely valuable and much less exhausting.

## 9. Awards and Recognition

Recognition is free and it is the most underused retention tool available.

| Award | Criteria |
|---|---|
| **Commendation** | Outstanding performance in a single operation |
| **Instructor Ribbon** | 10 successful certifications delivered |
| **Campaign Ribbon** | Participation in a completed campaign phase |
| **Wound Marker** | Serious casualty sustained in an operation |
| **Sponsor Ribbon** | 5 sponsored recruits reaching Specialist |
| **Unit Citation** | Battalion-level exceptional performance |
| **Meritorious Service** | Sustained contribution over 6 months |

**Awards appear on the personnel file and as armor markings.** Present them in formation, out loud, with a specific reason. "For pulling two casualties out under fire at Sullust" beats a Discord ping every time.

**Recognize the unglamorous.** The person who ran the armory post reliably for three months deserves more recognition than the one who got a lucky kill in an op. Design your award practice around that.

## 10. The Officer's Checklist

Ask yourself weekly:

1. Did I correct anyone publicly this week? *(Don't.)*
2. Did anyone in my unit go a full week without being spoken to by name?
3. Is my 2IC ready to run this unit without me?
4. Who in my unit is closest to a promotion, and what specifically is holding them back?
5. Did I ask a subordinate for honest feedback and actually listen?
6. Did anyone leave my unit this month, and do I know why?
7. Am I still having fun? *(If no, take an LOA before you burn out and take three people with you.)*
